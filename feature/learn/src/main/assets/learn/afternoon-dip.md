Plenty of people feel their energy sag in the early or middle part of the afternoon. Lunch often gets the blame, but the dip shows up even when you skip the meal. It's part of your daily rhythm: sleep pressure has been building since morning, and your body clock's alerting signal eases off for a while before picking up again toward evening.

## What it feels like

The dip is usually mild when you're well rested and more noticeable when you're carrying sleep debt. Common signs include:

- heavy eyelids, or rereading the same paragraph;
- reaching for something sweet or another coffee;
- slower thinking in meetings and conversations.

## Working with it

Rather than pushing through, you can plan around it:

- Put demanding work in your morning or evening peak, and save routine tasks for the dip.
- Step outside for a short walk. Daylight and movement both give alertness a lift.
- If your day allows, a nap of 20 to 30 minutes early in the dip can help.
- Go easy on caffeine here, since a late cup can make it harder to fall asleep tonight.

A lighter lunch may also help, as a large, heavy meal can make the slump feel deeper.

## In Sleep Saver

Your energy schedule places the afternoon dip roughly six and a half to nine hours after your typical wake time. The app then checks your modeled energy curve for the day and slides the window toward its lowest point, by up to 45 minutes either way.

The dip moves with you. If your sleep times drift earlier or later over a week or two, the window follows.

If you switch on the **Nap window** habit, its reminder arrives at the start of the dip with a suggested nap of 20 to 30 minutes.
