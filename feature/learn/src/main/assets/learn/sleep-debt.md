Sleep debt is the gap between the sleep your body needs and the sleep you actually get, added up over recent nights. One short night is easy to shrug off. A run of them tends to show up as slower mornings, a shorter fuse and an afternoon that drags.

## How it builds

Say you need eight hours and get six and a half. That night leaves you an hour and a half short. Repeat it four nights in a row and the shortfall is no longer small, even if each night felt manageable at the time.

Thinking in terms of debt captures something a single nightly total hides: tiredness carries over. How you feel today reflects the past week or two, not only last night.

## Paying it back

Debt comes down when you sleep a little more than you need, or simply meet your need for a while. A few habits help:

- Keep a steady bedtime for several nights rather than counting on one long weekend sleep.
- Go to bed a bit earlier instead of sleeping in late, so your body clock stays put.
- On days when you're clearly behind, add a short early-afternoon nap.

One very long sleep rarely clears a large debt in one go, and a big lie-in can nudge your rhythm later. A few slightly longer nights usually work better.

## In Sleep Saver

Sleep Saver compares each of your last 14 nights with your sleep need. Recent nights count more: last night has full weight, and each earlier night counts a little less, so a rough night two weeks ago barely registers.

- A night shorter than your need adds to your debt.
- A night longer than your need pays some back, but one night can earn at most 2 hours of credit.
- Nights with no data are skipped and listed, so you can fill them in.

Because older nights keep fading, your debt also eases a little on days when you sleep about your need.

Debt under 2 hours shows as **low**, 2 to 5 hours as **moderate**, and 5 hours or more as **high**. Every day you stay under 5 hours adds to your steady streak.
