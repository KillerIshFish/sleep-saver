Plenty of things are said to help you sleep. Some have been tested well, some only a little, and some hardly at all. None of them works like a switch, and a calm, unhurried evening matters more than doing everything right. Here is roughly where the research stands, in plain words.

## Good evidence

- A warm shower or bath, an hour or two before bed. Warming up helps your body shed heat afterwards, and that drop in temperature comes with sleepiness.
- Putting screens away. Bright light late in the evening holds back melatonin and can push your rhythm later.
- A cool, dark room. A hot bedroom makes sleep lighter and more broken.
- A body scan: moving your attention slowly through your body without trying to change anything. Mindfulness practice has improved how rested people feel in several trials.

## Some evidence

There is some evidence for slow breathing with a longer out-breath, for tensing and then releasing one muscle group at a time, and for writing a short list of what you need to do tomorrow. In one sleep-lab study, people who wrote specific to-do lists fell asleep sooner than people who wrote about what they had already done.

## Early or little evidence

The evidence is still early for reading on paper, gentle stretching and journaling. They are quiet and screen-free, which is a good enough reason to try them.

Steady background noise has little evidence behind it. It can mask sudden sounds, but earplugs often work better.

## In Sleep Saver

**Night steps** is your checklist for the evening. Each step sits a set time before your target bedtime, and the list runs from the start of your wind-down to bedtime. Every step shows how well studied it is: Good, Some, Early or Little evidence. Slow tide, Body scan and Muscle release open guided versions that can run with the screen dimmed, and a Tomorrow list waits for you in the morning.

Treat the steps as invitations, not duties. Skipping an evening is normal, and evenings you switch off in your Week plan never count against you.
