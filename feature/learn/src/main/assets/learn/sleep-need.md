Your sleep need is the amount of sleep that lets you feel and function at your best, night after night. Most adults land somewhere between seven and nine hours, but the exact figure is personal. It's shaped largely by biology, so it isn't something you can train down with willpower.

## Why your number matters

Your need is the yardstick for everything else. Seven hours is plenty for someone who needs seven, and a shortfall for someone who needs eight and a half. Measuring yourself against a friend's routine, or against one "ideal" figure, can leave you running short without noticing.

Need can also shift over the years, and you may need a little more for a while when you're unwell or unusually active.

## Finding it

The clearest clue comes from mornings when nothing wakes you: no alarm, no early meeting, nobody at the bedroom door. Left to wake on your own, you tend to take roughly what you need.

A few things can blur the picture:

- The first free night after a demanding week is often extra long because you're catching up.
- A late night out pushes sleep later, which can shorten it even on a free morning.
- Travel, stress and illness can throw things off for a while.

That's why a pattern across many free nights says more than any single one.

## In Sleep Saver

Sleep Saver starts from 8 hours, and you can change your need at any time in settings.

Once you have at least 14 nights recorded, the app looks back over the last 60 for free mornings: days when you woke at least 45 minutes later than your typical weekday wake time. It sets aside the first recovery night after a short one, then takes the middle value of the rest, rounded to 5 minutes.

With at least four free nights, you'll see a suggested need along with how confident the estimate is. It's only a suggestion. If you dismiss it, it comes back only once the estimate has moved by 15 minutes or more and at least a week has passed.
