On many mornings the hardest part of waking isn't the alarm itself but the fog that follows it. That fog is sleep inertia, and how thick it feels depends partly on what your brain was doing when the alarm went off.

## Why some wake-ups feel heavier

Through the night you move through cycles of deeper and shallower sleep. Deep sleep is when your brain is hardest to rouse: its activity is slow and widespread, and it takes a while to come back up to speed. Pulled out of it suddenly, you're more likely to feel confused and slow for the first part of the morning. Toward the end of a cycle, sleep is shallower, you shift and turn more, and waking usually takes less effort.

## What a phone can and can't tell

A phone can't read your brain waves, so it can't know which stage of sleep you're in. What it can notice, lying on the mattress, is movement. People tend to move more when sleep is shallow and less when it's deep, so a burst of movement close to your wake time is a fair hint that you're near the surface. It is only a hint:

- Lying still doesn't always mean deep sleep, and moving doesn't always mean you're about to wake.
- A partner, a pet or a very soft mattress can blur the signal.
- A phone that isn't on the bed can't feel anything at all.

## In Sleep Saver

With a **Surfacing alarm**, the phone only pays attention to movement near the end of your night. During the surfacing window, which you can set up to 45 minutes before your wake time, it may ring at a restless moment. If no clear moment comes, it rings at your wake time, never later. On nights when you're short on sleep the window widens a little, because a gentler start matters more then.

A **Wake-up style** sets how the alarm builds, from a slow, soft swell to a firm start. Place the phone face down on the mattress, away from your pillow. Never put it under pillows or covers. **Alarm check** looks for phone settings that could stop the alarm, and a **Test ring** lets you hear it before you rely on it. Once your watch has synced, **Wake landing** in the morning review shows where the alarm fell on the watch's estimate of your night.
