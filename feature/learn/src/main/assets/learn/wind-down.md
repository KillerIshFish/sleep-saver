Sleep rarely arrives on command. Going straight from a busy evening to lights out often means lying awake with your thoughts still running. A wind-down is a buffer: time for your body and mind to slow down before bed.

## What a good routine looks like

There's no single right routine. What matters is that it's calm, fairly consistent and something you'll actually do. Ideas many people find helpful:

- Dim the lights and put work out of sight.
- Take a warm shower or bath.
- Read something on paper, or something light.
- Stretch gently, breathe slowly or listen to quiet music.
- Jot down tomorrow's to-dos so they're out of your head.
- Lay out clothes or pack your bag for the morning.

Doing the same few things in the same order turns them into a cue. Over time, simply starting the routine tells your body that sleep is on its way.

## What gets in the way

- Large meals and alcohol close to bedtime.
- Hard exercise late in the evening.
- Work email, stressful conversations or the news just before bed.
- Scrolling in bed with a bright screen.

None of these are off-limits. They just make the switch to sleep harder, so moving them a little earlier is usually enough.
