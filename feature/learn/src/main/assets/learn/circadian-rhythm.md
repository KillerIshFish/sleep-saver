Your body runs on an internal clock that repeats about every 24 hours. It nudges body temperature, hormones, appetite and alertness up and down through the day. That's why you can feel sharp at one hour and foggy at another after the same night's sleep.

## Two forces at work

How awake you feel at any moment is roughly a balance of two things:

- **Sleep pressure** builds the longer you're awake and drains away while you sleep.
- **The circadian signal** rises and falls on its own schedule, whatever you're doing.

Through most of the day, the clock's alerting signal climbs to offset the growing pressure. In the evening it fades, pressure wins, and sleep comes more easily. Many people also notice a natural dip in the early afternoon, when the clock's push briefly eases.

## Keeping the clock steady

The clock sets itself mainly by light. Meals, movement and social routine add smaller cues. It works best when those cues are consistent:

- Wake at a similar time each day, weekends included where you can.
- Get daylight early and keep your evenings dimmer.
- If you need to shift your schedule, move it gradually, by 15 to 30 minutes every few days.

Big swings, such as sleeping in three hours on Saturday, act a little like crossing time zones. This is often called *social jet lag*, and it helps explain why Monday mornings can feel so heavy.
