Feeling slow and foggy right after you wake up is normal. It's known as sleep inertia: a transition period while your brain shifts from sleep into full wakefulness. Reaction time, memory and mood can lag behind for a while, even after a good night.

## What makes it heavier

Grogginess often lifts within 15 to 60 minutes, though it can linger longer. It tends to be stronger when:

- you're carrying a lot of sleep debt;
- an alarm pulls you out of deep sleep;
- you wake much earlier than your body clock expects.

Repeatedly hitting snooze breaks the end of the night into short pieces, so many people find a single alarm at a steady time easier to wake to.

## Helping it pass

You can't skip this phase entirely, but you can make it gentler:

- Open the curtains or step outside. Bright light is one of the strongest wake-up signals you have.
- Move a little: a short walk, a shower or a few stretches.
- Drink some water, and eat breakfast when you're ready.
- If you enjoy coffee, a morning cup can help. Just keep your afternoon cutoff in mind.

Where you can, save tasks that need sharp focus for after this first stretch of the day.

## In Sleep Saver

The first block of your energy schedule is grogginess. It begins when you actually woke up, or at your typical wake time until last night's data arrives, and lasts from about an hour to an hour and a half.

The more sleep debt you carry, the longer the block, because inertia tends to hit harder when you're behind on sleep. Your morning peak never starts before grogginess ends.

If the block often feels long, bringing your debt down is usually the most effective way to shorten it.
