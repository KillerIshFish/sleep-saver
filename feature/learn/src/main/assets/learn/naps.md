A nap is one of the simplest ways to top up your energy on a day when you're short on sleep. Done well, it leaves you refreshed. Too late or too long, and it can leave you groggy and make tonight's sleep harder to find.

## Short and early

For most people the sweet spot is 10 to 20 minutes. That's long enough to feel the benefit and short enough that you're unlikely to sink into deep sleep, which is what makes waking from a long nap feel so heavy. A 30-minute nap can still help, but it can leave you groggy at first.

Timing matters as much as length. The best moment is usually the afternoon dip, when your body is already inclined to rest. Later in the day, a nap uses up some of the sleep pressure you need to fall asleep at bedtime.

## Tips for a better nap

- Set an alarm so a short nap stays short, and allow a few minutes to drift off.
- Lie down somewhere dim and quiet, or use an eye mask.
- Don't worry if you don't fully drop off. Resting quietly still helps.
- Give yourself a few minutes after waking before anything demanding.

Naps aren't for everyone. If you find they make it harder to fall asleep at night, skip them and protect your night-time sleep instead.

## In Sleep Saver

Naps count toward your sleep. Any sleep shorter than 3 hours that starts between 9 am and 8 pm is treated as a nap and added to that day's total, so it also helps pay down your sleep debt. If your watch missed one, you can add it by hand.

**Catnap** on the Tonight tab sets a 10, 20 or 30 minute nap alarm, with 5 extra minutes to fall asleep, and tells you whether now is a good moment for a nap. The **Nap window** habit is off by default. Switch it on and you'll get a reminder at the start of your afternoon dip.
