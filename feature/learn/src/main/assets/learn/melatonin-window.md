Toward the end of the day there's a stretch when sleep comes more easily. Sleep pressure is high, your body clock is winding down, and your body is getting ready for night. Going to bed inside that stretch usually means less tossing and turning.

## Why timing matters

Get into bed well before you're sleepy and you may lie awake. Push much later and you can hit a second wind, when the clock's evening alertness keeps you going and sleep gets harder to catch. Then a fixed alarm cuts the night short.

The name comes from melatonin, a hormone your body releases as evening turns to night. Its levels usually start rising a couple of hours before your habitual sleep time, and bright evening light can hold that rise back. In Sleep Saver the name is a nod to that shift toward night. The app doesn't measure hormones: think of this as your best window to fall asleep.

## Making the most of it

- Start winding down an hour or so beforehand, so you're ready when the window opens.
- Keep lights low and screens dim as it approaches.
- If you're not sleepy yet, wait for the window rather than lying in bed watching the clock.
- If you miss it, don't worry. Go to bed when you next feel sleepy, and keep tomorrow's wake time steady.

## In Sleep Saver

The melatonin window spans the hour around your target bedtime, 30 minutes either side. Your target bedtime is worked out backwards from tomorrow's typical wake time: minus your sleep need, minus 15 minutes to drift off.

The **Bedtime** reminder arrives as the window opens, and your wind-down block sits just before it. Treat the window as a guide rather than a deadline. Anywhere inside it is a good time to turn in.
