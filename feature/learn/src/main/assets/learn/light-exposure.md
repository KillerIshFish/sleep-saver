title: Light and your body clock
summary: Morning daylight sets your clock for the day. Bright evening light pushes it later.

Light is the strongest signal your body clock receives. Specialized cells in your eyes report how bright it is directly to the clock, and the timing of that light tells your body when the day begins and ends.

## Morning light

Bright light soon after waking helps anchor your rhythm. It tends to make you feel alert sooner, and over time it helps you feel sleepy at a sensible hour in the evening.

- Aim for 10 to 30 minutes outside within the first hour or so after waking.
- An overcast sky is still usually far brighter than indoor lighting.
- A walk, breakfast by an open door or a commute on foot all count.
- If getting outside isn't possible, sit close to a bright window.

You never need to look at the sun. Simply being out in daylight is enough.

## Evening light

The same sensitivity works the other way after dark. Bright light late in the evening tells your clock it's still daytime, which can push your sleepiness later.

- Switch to lamps and warmer, dimmer lighting in the last couple of hours before bed.
- Turn screen brightness down, and use a night mode if your devices have one.
- Keep your bedroom dark, or try an eye mask.

You don't need to sit in darkness all evening. The goal is dimmer, not perfect.

## In Sleep Saver

The **Morning light** habit is on by default. Its reminder comes 15 minutes after your typical wake time rather than your actual wake time, because sleep data from your watch can take a while to arrive in the morning.

In the evening, the **Dim the lights** habit reminds you as your wind-down begins, about 90 minutes before your target bedtime. You can move either reminder in the habit settings.
